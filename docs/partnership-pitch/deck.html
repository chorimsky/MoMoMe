The strategic opportunity

A wallet balance is only as useful as the places it can land.

Sorted holds value for its users. In Cameroon, the place that value has to reach is a Mobile Money number — MTN or Orange — because that is what rent, school fees and a market trader accept. The distance between those two facts is the opportunity.

What Sorted owns

The customer, the balance, the product experience, and the trust that comes with being the app someone already uses.

What the last mile costs

Operator onboarding, prefunded float per carrier, payout and collection adapters, FX, reconciliation, compliance, and an on-call rota for when a vendor fails. Each one is a quarter of work before a single payment lands.

What a gateway changes

Sorted integrates once. The rails, routing, retries, settlement and reconciliation sit behind a signed API and a webhook contract that does not change when a rail does.

This deck describes an unexercised partnership. Sorted would be MoMo›Me's first wallet partner; no claim of existing wallet integrations is made anywhere in this document.

The connectivity challenge

The hard part is not the payment. It is everything that happens when one fails.

Mobile Money does not reverse. A payout that leaves is gone, and a payout that silently does not leave is worse — because the balance has already been debited somewhere. Most integration effort goes here, not into the happy path.

Four questions every integration has to answer

  • Did it actually land? A provider callback is a hint, not proof.
  • Is the rail down, or is our balance zero? Opposite responses; identical symptom.
  • Is this the right person? A mistyped digit is a stranger with your money.
  • Can we prove it, a month later? Reconciliation is what a regulator asks for.

Why it compounds

  • Each operator and aggregator answers differently, and changes without notice.
  • Float is prefunded per carrier — an MTN balance tells you nothing about Orange.
  • Quotes expire; a rate held too long is a loss, held too briefly is a failed payment.
  • A vendor can disappear. This month, one of ours did.

The last point is not rhetorical, and it is addressed directly on slide 14.

What it does today

Takes a crypto or stablecoin payment in, locks an FX rate for a bounded window, routes to a funded Mobile Money rail, settles to an MTN or Orange number, and reconciles all three legs continuously.

How a rail is added

Implement an adapter interface and append it to a registry array. Routing, health tracking, failover, idempotency and reconciliation are inherited, not rewritten. A bank or MFI with a request/response API is the same shape.

Why MoMo›Me is the right strategic fit

Four properties that are difficult to retrofit, and are already load-bearing here.

Unknown is never treated as zero

A balance we cannot read returns null, never 0. The router refuses rather than guesses, and the operator is told which of the two it was. Most integrations collapse these, and send people hunting an empty wallet when the real fault was a dead host.

Settlement follows proof, not notification

A provider callback is a hint that prompts an authoritative re-query. Only that re-query can complete a payment. A forged or replayed callback cannot release money.

A superseded attempt cannot settle

When a payout fails over to a second rail, a late verdict from the first is ignored. Without this, a refund can be issued against money another rail is still carrying — a real defect, found and fixed with a regression test that fails against the old code.

The recipient is checked before the money moves

Recipient verification returns the operator-registered name behind a number, through whichever provider can answer — independent of which rail will settle the payment.

Each property is covered by automated tests in the release gate. Sources are listed in the evidence register accompanying this deck.

The partnership architecture

One boundary, drawn so that our problems never become Sorted's problems.

Sorted's responsibility

  • Custody of user balances and the in-app experience
  • Initiating a payment intent with a destination and amount
  • Receiving and acting on webhook state changes
  • First-line user support, using the reference we return

MoMo›Me's responsibility

  • Recipient resolution, quoting and FX rate lock
  • Rail selection, retries and failover across payout rails
  • Settlement to the Mobile Money number, and its proof
  • Reconciliation, compliance screening and the audit record

The contract at the boundary

A signed /v1 API with per-organisation credentials and scopes; idempotency keys on every money-moving call; HMAC-signed webhooks with retries and a dead-letter queue; and a stable status vocabulary that does not change when a rail is added, replaced or fails. When a vendor of ours disappears, Sorted's integration does not change. That is the whole point of the boundary, and it was tested in production this month.

Go to market

Sorted's channel, our rails — launched into one corridor before any other.

Who we start with

A bounded cohort of Sorted users in Cameroon, cash-out only, with per-transaction and daily caps set jointly. Small enough to watch every payment; large enough to prove the economics.

How it reaches users

Inside Sorted, as a destination in a flow the user already understands. No new app, no new credential, no redirect. We are infrastructure and should be invisible.

What we provide

A sandbox with deterministic scenario numbers, an API reference, a partner hub for the integrating team, and a named operational contact for the pilot window.

What success at this stage looks like

Not volume. A pilot succeeds if every payment reaches a terminal state we can explain, every failure has a reason the user could act on, and the month reconciles to the last franc across all three legs.

Risk, trust and governance

What happened this month, and what it tells you about the system.

A partner deck that omits this would be discovered in diligence. We would rather present it, because the behaviour under failure is the strongest thing we have to show.

The failure

On 24 October a payout vendor withdrew its API hostnames from DNS without notice. A second rail was unfunded. For four days MoMo›Me could not settle a Mobile Money payout at all. Recipient verification, which used the same vendor, went dark with it.

What held

No payment was lost, stranded or misdirected. The flow refused every method before taking money, told the sender nothing had been charged, and disabled the confirm button. Unknown was never read as zero; no payout was released without an authoritative re-query.

What it exposed — and what changed

The defects were in how the failure was described, not in how money was handled. The sender was told "no funded payout rail" when the truth was that no rail could be reached; the console reported an unreachable rail as operational. Both were fixed and deployed on 28 October, each with a regression test that fails against the previous behaviour. The vendor has since issued a new gateway and is being reconnected.

Current status, 28 Oct 2026: one payout rail live and authenticating, float unfunded; one rail migrating; one inactive. Recipient verification restored and returning names.

Strategic expansion

Cameroon is a corridor, not the thesis.

More destinations

A bank or MFI with a request/response API is the same adapter shape as an aggregator. Routing, reconciliation and failover are inherited. The constraint is licensing posture, not protocol.

More corridors

Francophone markets share operators, currency behaviour and regulatory contours. Each is an adapter plus a compliance review, not a rebuild.

More origins

The pay-in side is its own registry. New assets and networks attach without touching the settlement path Sorted integrates against.

What we are explicit about

Connecting to a national switch requires licensed participation, held either by us or by a sponsoring institution. We hold no such posture today, and this deck claims none. It is a decision to take together, with eyes open, not a capability to assume.

The partnership proposal

What we are asking Sorted to consider.

We propose

  • A technology and settlement partnership, not a software licence
  • Scope one: cash-out from Sorted to MTN and Orange in Cameroon
  • A bounded pilot under jointly agreed caps
  • A negotiated per-transaction rate fixed before launch
  • Joint monthly review against the measures on slide 15

We are asking Sorted for

  • A named technical and a named commercial counterpart
  • Expected volume and average transfer size, to model economics honestly
  • Agreement on the pilot cohort and its caps
  • Confirmation of Sorted's compliance requirements for a settlement partner
  • A decision on Phase 1 — scope, not commitment

What we commit to before any live payment

A funded payout float with per-carrier balances visible to both parties; a rehearsed failover drill; and written confirmation of rail status on the day of launch. We will not ask Sorted to send a user's money through a rail we cannot currently reach.
